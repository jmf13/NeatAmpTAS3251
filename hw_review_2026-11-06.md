### P0-2 · I²C bus on ESP32-S3 strapping pins IO0 and IO45 ✅ hardware, firmware open (downgraded: IO45 was not a real risk on N8R8)
- **Where (V1.0–V1.2):** SDA = IO0 (U6 pin 27), SCL = IO45 (U6 pin 26), with 6.8 kΩ pull-ups R21/R22 on the Amplifier page
- **Re-checked 2026-11-21 against the ESP32-S3-WROOM-1/1U datasheet v1.8:**
  - **IO45 (VDD_SPI strap): no problem on the N8R8.** §8 Module Schematics: *"For modules with PSRAM, the VDD_SPI voltage is fixed to 3.3 V or 1.8 V via eFuse, so their VDD_SPI voltage will not be affected by the GPIO45 level."* The original "not guaranteed across batches" claim was wrong. It only matters for modules without PSRAM (e.g. N4/N8/N16), where GPIO45 must not be pulled high at power-up.
  - **IO0 (boot-mode strap): small but real risk.** GPIO0 = 0 with GPIO46 = 0 (its default pull-down) at reset selects Joint Download Boot (Table 4-3). Normal boot is fine thanks to the SDA pull-up. But if the ESP32 resets (watchdog, brown-out, reset button) in the middle of an I²C read, U12 can be left driving SDA low while SCL idles high. The ESP32 then sits in download mode until power-cycled.
  - IO0 on the I²C bus also blocks a BOOT button (P2-4).
- **Fix (done in schematic 2026-11-21):** SDA → **IO47** (pin 24), SCL → **IO21** (pin 23). Other pins moved to make room: DSP_RESET → IO48 (pin 25), DSP_SELFBOOT → IO14 (pin 22), ESP_TAS_ADR → IO13 (pin 21). IO0 and IO45 are now unconnected. IO47/IO48 run at 3.3 V on the N8R8 (only R16V modules have 1.8 V on these pins).
- **V1.0 boards:** no action needed for IO45. A board stuck in download mode after a warm reset recovers on a power cycle.

### P0-4 · Inrush current: 6600 µF on PVDD behind a fast-acting fuse
- **Where:** C4 (Power 12V page) + C54, C55 (Amplifier page) = 3 × 2200 µF / 63 V; F1 = Littelfuse 0451005 (fast-acting); Q1 reverse-polarity P-FET turns on within a fraction of a millisecond
- **Problem:** hot-plugging a stiff supply can blow the fuse or slowly weaken it.
- **Fix (pick one or more):**
  - [x] ~~Add 100 nF–1 µF from Q1 gate to source (soft-start)~~ **Rejected (2026-11-17).** Once Q1 is corrected for reverse polarity (P0-6), its body diode conducts input → PVDD at plug-in. The bulk caps then charge through the diode before the gate does anything, so a gate cap gives no inrush limiting. On V1.0 (Q1 the wrong way round) it would delay turn-on, but Q1 would then dissipate the full ½·C·V² ≈ 4.3 J (6600 µF at 36 V) in its linear region. That's too risky for a 5×6 DFN without an SOA check.
  - [ ] Replace F1 with a **time-lag (slow-blow)** fuse in the same NANO² footprint
  - [x] ~~Reduce the bulk capacitance (see P1-3)~~ **Rejected (2026-11-21):** keep 3 × 2200 µF for enough energy reserve on PVDD. Inrush has to be handled by the fuse type or a limiter instead.
  - [ ] For real inrush limiting together with reverse-polarity protection: an NTC in series, or a hot-swap / ideal-diode controller with a separate inrush FET (next revision)
- **V1.0 procedure:** power up from a current-limited bench supply. Don't hot-plug a stiff supply until F1 is a time-lag type.

### P0-6 · Q1 reverse-polarity P-FET mounted source-to-input ✅
- **Where:** Power 12V page, Q1 E38P100KH (P-channel, VDS −100 V, VGS ±20 V; pads S = 1,2,3, G = 4, D = 5–8 + EP), with D1 (15 V Zener) and R2 between input and gate, and R5 from gate to GND
- **Problem:** V1.0, V1.1 and the EasyEDA V1.2 design have **S on the input (F1) side and D on PVDD**. With correct polarity, Vgs ≈ −15 V (clamped by D1), so Q1 is on and everything works. With a **reversed** supply, Q1's body diode (anode D, cathode S) conducts from the board side into the input, D2 conducts forward, and only F1 blowing protects the board.
- **Fix:** **D to the input (F1), S to PVDD**, and D1 cathode + R2 on the source (PVDD) side. At plug-in the body diode conducts until Vgs turns the channel on. With a reversed supply the diode blocks and Vgs ≥ 0 keeps Q1 off. Done in the KiCad 10 redraw (`KiCADv10/Power_12V_DC.kicad_sch`, branch `hw/schematic-redraw`). Needs a PCB change.
- **V1.0 patch:** none practical (DFN, pads on fixed sides). Check the CN1 polarity before connecting.
- [x] Schematic (KiCad 10)
- [x] PCB: the KiCad 10 board is built from the corrected schematic, so Q1's pads carry the corrected nets (S pads 1–3 = PVDD, D pads 5–9 = F1 side). Schematic parity is 0. Route Q1 according to the ratsnest; don't copy the V1.2 copper.

## P1 – Savings

### How to move parts to cheaper options in the JLCPCB flow

1. **Know the part classes.** In the JLCPCB parts library, **Basic** parts are preloaded and have no per-part loading fee. **Extended** parts carry a feeder-loading fee per *unique* part number. Check jlcpcb.com/parts for the current fee rules and the "Preferred Extended" category.
2. **Find the Extended parts.** In EasyEDA Pro, export the BOM and, in the JLCPCB part library search, filter by **"Basic"**. Any LCSC number not found under Basic is Extended.
3. **Replace in EasyEDA Pro.** Select the component → *Replace Component* (or right-click → *Update/Replace*) → in the library panel choose *JLCPCB Assembly / LCSC*, filter for **Basic**, and match value, footprint, tolerance and voltage. To change every instance of a part at once, use *Find Similar Objects* → multi-select → replace.
4. **Consolidate values.** Fewer unique part numbers means fewer Extended fees. Use one 1 µF part, one 100 nF part and one resistor brand for everything.
5. **Mark hand-soldered parts** (MA5172, film caps, bulk electrolytics, terminal blocks, headers) as *Exclude from BOM / Do not place* for JLC assembly, so they aren't quoted or sourced.
6. **Re-run DRC** after footprint changes, regenerate the BOM and CPL, and check the part rotations in JLC's preview before ordering.

## P2 – Improvements

### P2-1 · Filter the analog LDO input (TAS3251 AVDD)
- **Where:** U5 TPS7A2033, fed from +5V, which is shared with two switching regulators
- **Change:** add a **ferrite bead (0603, ~600 Ω @ 100 MHz) + 4.7–10 µF** between +5V and U5's IN. Return the new capacitor to **GND** on the bead's input side; keep C42 on **AGND**.
- [ ] Schematic + PCB

### P2-2 · Analog input coupling capacitors
- **Where:** C57–C60 (10 µF X7R 25 V 1210) between the DAC output network and the power-stage inputs
- **Change:** 50 V X7R in the same 1210 footprint (much less capacitance loss under bias), or film / bipolar electrolytic if THD measurements show a rise at low frequencies
- [ ] Measure THD vs frequency before and after

### P2-3 · +5V rail is ~4.6 V (diode OR-ing D5/D3)
- **Option A:** R9 12.4 k → **11.3 k** (≈5.4 V before the diode, ≈5.0 V after)
- **Option B:** move the top of the feedback divider (R8) to the +5V side of D5, so the loop corrects for the diode drop
- **Option C:** ideal-diode / P-FET OR-ing (no drop)
- **Side effect:** with a true 5.0 V, the SK9822's VIH ≈ 3.5 V > 3.3 V GPIO. Add a **74AHCT1G125** (or similar) on the data and clock lines, or keep the LEDs on the diode-dropped rail.
- Also gives the AP63203 (3.3 V buck) more input headroom.
- [ ] Decide on an option

### P2-4 · Add a BOOT button on IO0
- IO0 is free since P0-2 and now sits on **H2 pin 8, next to GND on pin 7** (2026-11-21). A jumper or tweezers across 7–8 while pressing reset (SW1) enters download mode, so a separate button is optional.
- Do **not** tie IO0 to GND permanently: IO0 = 0 at every reset means the board always starts in download mode and never runs the firmware. Leave it to the internal weak pull-up.
- [x] Schematic (via H2)
- [ ] Optional: a real BOOT button (IO0 → GND) if the jumper is too fiddly

### P2-5 · Buck converter mode and noise
- If P0-5 shows the LMQ66430s are **not** in FPWM: use the FPWM variant or drive MODE/SYNC correctly.
- TPS7A4701 is premium for a gate-driver rail. Consider a cheaper LDO **only after** FPWM is confirmed.

### P2-7 · Strapping pin hygiene
- Since `c79f2cb`, IO3 and IO46 only drive the SK9822 inputs (U8, via R17/R18), so they're no longer exposed on H2. The LED inputs don't pull either pin, so the strap defaults hold (IO46 low, IO3 only matters if the JTAG eFuses are burned).
