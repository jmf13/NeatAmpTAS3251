### P0-4 · Inrush current: 6600 µF on PVDD behind a fast-acting fuse ✅ fuse changed, bench check open
- **Where:** C4 (Power 12V page) + C54, C55 (Amplifier page) = 3 × 2200 µF / 63 V; F1 = Littelfuse 0451005 (fast-acting); Q1 reverse-polarity P-FET turns on within a fraction of a millisecond

The bulk caps then charge through the diode before the gate does anything, so a gate cap gives no inrush limiting. On V1.0 (Q1 the wrong way round) it would delay turn-on, but Q1 would then dissipate the full ½·C·V² ≈ 4.3 J (6600 µF at 36 V) in its linear region.   - [x] Replace F1 with a **time-lag (slow-blow)** fuse in the same NANO² footprint. **Done 2026-11-22:** F1 = **Littelfuse 0452005.MRL, LCSC C66503** (NANO² Slo-Blo, 5 A, 125 V, 50 A interrupt, melting I²t 53.7 A²s, 2410 / 6.1 × 2.5 mm, same footprint `FUSE-SMD_L6.1-W2.6`). The PSU's own soft-start and current limit bound the inrush, and the time-lag fuse handles it. A controller would also move the ½·C·V² ≈ 4.3 J into an inrush FET, which then needs an SOA-rated part and a ramp of at least 300 ms. Not worth it for this product. The old 0451005 (fast-acting) sat close to its limit and would slowly age.

## P1 – Savings

### How to move parts to cheaper options in the JLCPCB flow

1. **Know the part classes.** In the JLCPCB parts library, **Basic** parts are preloaded and have no per-part loading fee. **Extended** parts carry a feeder-loading fee per *unique* part number. Check jlcpcb.com/parts for the current fee rules and the "Preferred Extended" category.
2. **Find the Extended parts.** In EasyEDA Pro, export the BOM and, in the JLCPCB part library search, filter by **"Basic"**. Any LCSC number not found under Basic is Extended.
3. **Replace in EasyEDA Pro.** Select the component → *Replace Component* (or right-click → *Update/Replace*) → in the library panel choose *JLCPCB Assembly / LCSC*, filter for **Basic**, and match value, footprint, tolerance and voltage. To change every instance of a part at once, use *Find Similar Objects* → multi-select → replace.
4. **Consolidate values.** Fewer unique part numbers means fewer Extended fees. Use one 1 µF part, one 100 nF part and one resistor brand for everything.
5. **Mark hand-soldered parts** (MA5172, film caps, bulk electrolytics, terminal blocks, headers) as *Exclude from BOM / Do not place* for JLC assembly, so they aren't quoted or sourced.
6. **Re-run DRC** after footprint changes, regenerate the BOM and CPL, and check the part rotations in JLC's preview before ordering.

### P2-3 · +5V rail is ~4.6 V (diode OR-ing D5/D3) ✅ decided: keep 4.6 V
- **Topology:** U3 (LMQ66430, 5.02 V) → D5 and USB-C VBUS → L6 → D3 are OR-ed into the **+5V** net. D3/D5 are **SS34** (3 A, 40 V Schottky, C8678); the "1N5819" symbol name is wrong (P2-8). At typical load the drop is ~0.35–0.45 V, so **+5V ≈ 4.6 V** (≈ 4.4–4.8 V from USB-C, depending on VBUS and the cable). Read "+5V" in this review and in the README as "~4.6 V".
- **Options considered:**
  - **A:** R9 12.4 k → 11.3 k. U3 outputs 5.42 V, so +5V ≈ 5.0–5.2 V after D5. The divider stays before D5 (49.9 k ∥ 11.3 k = 9.2 kΩ, within the 5–10 kΩ detection rule).
  - **B:** move the top of the feedback divider (R8) after D5. **Rejected** (2026-11-25, LMQ664x0-Q1 SNVSBV1F):
    - *Start-up detection:* U3 picks fixed 3.3 V or adjustable output *"by sensing the resistance of the feedback path during start-up"* (§8.2.1.2.2.1). With USB-C plugged in before PVDD (the normal development case), the FB pin is pre-biased to ~4.7 V × 12.4/62.3 ≈ 0.94 V. The datasheet doesn't specify detection with a pre-biased pin. If U3 misdetects fixed 3.3 V, it regulates the FB node to 3.3 V, so **+5V → ~16 V**. That destroys U5 (6.5 V abs. max), the SK9822 LEDs, and whatever hangs on the U11 +5V pin.
    - *Loop stability:* D5 (a few Ω dynamic resistance at light load) and C105/C106 (2 × 47 µF) would sit inside the loop. That adds a ~1 kHz pole in the feedback path, far below the crossover of U3's internal compensation, and a feed-forward cap can't fix it because C_FF is limited by the same detection circuit.
  - **C:** ideal-diode OR instead of D3/D5. True 5.0 V, no drop, and no 0.5–0.7 W diode loss (see P2-10). Needs a new part and a layout change.
- **Side effect of any true 5.0 V:** the SK9822 input-high threshold is 0.7 × VDD = 3.5 V, above the 3.3 V GPIO. It would need a 74AHCT1G125 (or similar) on data and clock. At 4.6 V it's 3.22 V, which the GPIO just meets.
- **What needs 5 V?** Nothing on this board: AP63203 needs VIN ≥ 3.8 V (UVLO rising 3.5 V typ), TPS7A2033 ≥ 3.45 V, SK9822 runs at 4.6 V. The DSP board (ADAU1466 + XU316) runs from 3.3 V. The U11 I²S module gets ~4.6 V on pin 2; check that it has its own 3.3 V regulator.
- [x] **Decided 2026-11-25: option 1, keep +5V at ~4.6 V. No change.** Option A stays available if an external module turns out to need a real 5 V; option C is a candidate for the next revision if D5's heat matters (P2-10).

**Findings:**
1. **U4 is fine for the ESP32 + TAS3251 + a typical DSP program (~1.1 A), but has no margin for a heavy DSP program**: ~1.55 A with a buck for the XU316 core, ≈ 2 A in the ADAU1466 hot worst case, and **≈ 2.2 A (over the rating) with an LDO** for the XU316 core. If a Wi-Fi burst pushes U4 into its peak limit for 2 ms, it hiccups and **the ESP32 and the DSP reset together**.
   → **Recommendation (A24): power the DSP board from +5V (≈ 4.6 V), not from 3.3V.** U11 pin 2 (the I²S connector the DSP board already uses) carries +5V, and JST XH is rated 3 A per pin. The DSP board then makes its own 3.3 V, 1.2 V, 0.9 V and 1.8 V (bucks for 3.3 V, 1.2 V and 0.9 V; 1.8 V can be an LDO; see the architecture below), and U4 only carries the ESP32 + TAS3251 (~0.55 A). The 3.3V on U10/CN5 is then only used for the I²C pull-ups/logic reference on the DSP board.
   → On the DSP board, feed the ADAU1466 DVDD from a **1.2 V buck** (VDRIVE → GND) instead of the PNP: that saves 0.7–1.0 W of PNP heat and ~60 % of the ADAU1466 input current. With the PNP, keep IOVDD-first sequencing in mind if DVDD comes from a separate supply.
   → The total power still comes from U3 either way (finding 5).
2. **Input headroom: OK.** At 4.6 V the AP63203 needs ~72 % duty plus I·R drops; with low-dropout mode it still regulates down to VIN ≈ 3.3 V + I × (125 mΩ + L5 DCR), i.e. ≈ 3.6 V at 1.5 A. +5V from USB-C (≈ 4.3–4.8 V) is also fine.
3. **Output capacitance below the datasheet recommendation.** 2 × 10 µF 1210 50 V X7R (~9 µF each at 3.3 V) = ~18 µF ceramic vs 44 µF recommended, plus the 47 µF electrolytic. With 0.3–0.5 A load steps (Wi-Fi bursts, DSP start-up) the dip will be larger than necessary. → Add **one more 10 µF** (same C138687, no new part), or change C39/C50 to 22 µF (A23).
4. **PFM at light load.** Only relevant when the ESP32 sleeps and the DSP board is off. With the ESP32 running (≥ 40–100 mA), U4 should be in CCM. If PFM bursts show up on DAC_DVDD or as audible noise, the **AP63201** (stays in CCM, 500 kHz, adjustable) is the fix: same TSOT26 pinout, but it needs a feedback divider instead of FB tied to VOUT (R1 196 k / R2 62 k → 3.33 V; the datasheet's 182 k / 62 k row gives only 3.15 V) and the 6.8 µH inductor that L5 already is. Plan two 0402 pads if you want the option (check at T14).
5. **U3 and D5 thermal at 1.5 A on +5V.** +5V load ≈ U4 input (1.4 A × 3.3 / (4.6 × 0.88) ≈ 1.15 A at 1.4 A out) + SK9822 (≤ 0.12 A) + U5 (~0.03 A) + U11 module (~0.05–0.1 A) ≈ **1.3–1.5 A** (up to ~1.7 A with an XU316 LDO).
   - **D5:** 0.45 V × 1.5 A ≈ **0.7 W** in an SMA package (θJA typically 75–100 °C/W on minimum pads) → **+50–70 °C**. Give D5's anode/cathode copper enough area. This is the strongest argument for option C (ideal-diode OR) in a later revision.
   - **U3:** 5 V × 1.5 A = 7.5 W out. At 35 V → 5 V and 2.2 MHz (at the minimum on-time) the efficiency is estimated at ~80–85 % (no datasheet curve for 35 V / 5 V; **measure**), so ≈ 1.3–1.9 W loss. With RθJA ≈ 45–50 °C/W (TI EVM / 4-layer) that's **+60–95 °C**. Inside the enclosure, next to the U12 heatsink, TJ could approach the 150 °C limit. → Measure U3 temperature with the DSP board running (T14); the thermal vias and GND copper around U3 matter.
6. **USB-C only (programming mode):** CC has 5.1 kΩ Rd (R12/R13), so a PC port supplies only **500 mA** at 5 V (USB 2.0 default) ≈ **0.6 A at 3.3 V**. That's enough for the ESP32 alone, **not** for ESP32 + ADAU1466 + XU316: the port current-limits or VBUS sags and the ESP32 browns out. → With USB-C only, keep the DSP board unplugged, or use a USB-C charger that advertises ≥ 1.5 A (Type-C current).
7. **DSP board input:** its supply arrives through a cable (U11 pin 2 +5V if A24 is adopted, otherwise U10/CN5 3.3 V). Put local bulk at the DSP board input (≥ 47–100 µF + ceramics) so its load steps don't travel back up the cable. On 3.3V, U4's 4 ms soft-start keeps the inrush of 200 µF extra bulk at ~0.17 A. On +5V there is no soft-start in front of the bulk, so give the DSP board's regulators a soft-start or an inrush limit; the hot-plug rule is the same as for the rest of the board (plug the DSP board in unpowered).
8. **Start-up order is fine:** U4 starts when +5V passes 3.5 V; the ESP32 EN RC (R52 10 kΩ × C48 1 µF ≈ 10 ms) releases the ESP32 after U4's 4 ms soft-start.
9. **L5 = 6.8 µH vs the recommended 3.9 µH** for the 1.1 MHz AP63203. 6.8 µH is inside the general 2.2–10 µH range and gives low ripple (~0.13 A at 4.6 V in, vs the 30–50 % of 2 A the datasheet designs for), but the internal compensation is tuned for 3.9 µH and the larger L slows the response to load steps. Keep it for V1.3 (Sunltech SLO0630 series like L2, already placed) and check the load-step dip at T14; if it's poor, change L5 to 3.9 µH (same footprint family).

#### DSP board power connector CN6 (A24, added 2026-11-26)

- **Part:** JST XH 4-pin, **B4B-XH-A(LF)(SN), LCSC C144395** (3 A per pin, keyed housing). It's the only 4-pin connector on the board, so it can't be mixed up with CN5 (3.3 V out, 2-pin XH) or the 6-pin XH signal connectors.
- **Pinout:** 1, 2 = **5V_PVDD**; 3, 4 = **GND**. Two pins per rail halve contact and cable resistance.
- **Tap point: U3 output, before D5.**
  - The DSP board gets ~5.0 V regulated by U3's feedback (R8 senses this net) instead of ~4.6 V after the diode.
  - D5 only carries the ESP32 side (~0.6 A, ≈ 0.3 W instead of ~0.7 W at 1.5 A), which relieves finding 5.
  - The DSP board inrush is covered by U3's soft-start.
- **Polarity:** no diode needed. The XH housing only fits one way; the remaining risk is a mis-crimped cable, so check it once.
- **Load:** the DSP board draws ≈ 0.4 A typical, ≈ 0.7 A max and ≈ 0.8 A in the ADAU1466 hot worst case (≈ 3 W in at 5 V with ~85 % converters).
- **USB-C only:** with PVDD off, 5V_PVDD is dead, so the DSP board is **off** automatically. A PC port can't overload (finding 6).
- **Firmware (back-powering):** while PVDD is absent (IO8 PVDD sense), keep I²S (CN8), DSP_RESET and DSP_SELFBOOT (CN7) **low or high-Z**. Otherwise the ESP32 feeds the unpowered ADAU1466/XU316 through their I/O protection diodes.
  - The I²C pull-ups R19/R20 are on the amp board's 3.3V and also idle high into the unpowered DSP board. Consider putting the DSP-side pull-ups on the DSP board's own 3.3 V instead.
- **Output capacitance:** the DSP board's input bulk sits on U3's output through the cable. Keep it moderate (47–100 µF + ceramics) and check it against the LMQ66430 maximum output capacitance.
- **Ground return:** the DSP board's supply current returns through CN6 pins 3–4, not through the signal GND of CN7/CN8. CN8 pin 2 (+5V after D5) can stay as a low-current pin or be left unused on the DSP board.

#### DSP board power architecture (A24, proposal 2026-11-25)

For a separate DSP board with the ADAU1466 and the XU316, fed from the amp board's **5V_PVDD (≈ 5.0 V) through CN6** (see above). The original idea was +5V through U11 pin 2.

**Principle:** one buck per high-current rail, **each fed directly from the 4.6 V input**. Don't make 3.3 V first and then the cores linearly from it (that just moves the U4 problem onto the DSP board). Never connect two buck outputs to the same rail: these converters don't share current. Several bucks on the same *input* line is fine; give each its own 10 µF input cap.

**Reusing the AP632xx family (same as U4):** yes, it fits.
- VIN 3.8–32 V (UVLO rising 3.5 V), so 4.4–5.25 V is fine. 2 A each, more than any single rail needs.
- **AP63203** (fixed 3.3 V, 1.1 MHz, LCSC C780769, already in the amp BOM) for 3.3 V.
- **AP63201** (adjustable, 500 kHz, **PWM only, no PFM**) for 1.2 V and 0.9 V. No light-load bursts in the audio band. The AP63200 (PFM version) is not recommended here.
- V_FB = 800 mV ±1 %, so 0.9 V and 1.2 V are both reachable. At 4.6 V in and 500 kHz the on-time is ≥ 0.39 µs, far above the 80 ns minimum.
- They have **no power-good pin**, but the EN pin has a precise threshold (1.18 V rising), which is enough for sequencing (below).

| Rail | Loads | Converter | Setting | Load (typ / max) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.3 V | ADAU1466 IOVDD (+ AVDD/PVDD via ferrite or small LDO); XU316 VDDIOL/R/T, USB_VDD33; QSPI flash, oscillator | **AP63203** | L 3.9 µH, C_OUT 2 × 22 µF | ~0.3 A / ~0.35 A |
| 1.2 V | ADAU1466 DVDD (VDRIVE → GND, no PNP) | **AP63201** | R1 30.9 k / R2 62 k → 1.199 V (Table 1); L 2.2 µH; C_OUT 2 × 22 µF | 0.22–0.33 A / 0.49 A (0.92 A hot worst case) |
| 0.9 V | XU316 VDD | **AP63201** | R1 7.68 k / R2 61.9 k → 0.899 V; L 2.2 µH; C_OUT ≥ 3 × 22 µF + ≥ 8 × 100 nF at the pins | 0.3 A / 1.0 A |
| 0.9 V PLL | XU316 PLL_AVDD | from 0.9 V via 600 Ω ferrite + 1 µF (XMOS) | | ≤ 5 mA |
| 1.8 V | XU316 VDDIOB18, OTP_VCC, USB_VDD18 | LDO from 3.3 V | | ~50 mA |

- **0.9 V accuracy:** XU316 VDD must stay within 0.855–0.945 V (±5 %), abs. max 1.05 V. Reference ±1 % plus 1 % resistors (the divider error counts only ~11 % at this ratio) gives about ±1.3 % DC, which leaves ~3.5 % for ripple and load steps. The core current steps by several hundred mA, so the output caps and the 500 kHz loop response matter: measure the dip on the first DSP board. If it's too large, add capacitance or use a faster (1–2 MHz) adjustable buck for this one rail.
- **Ripple:** with 2.2 µH at 500 kHz, ΔI_L ≈ 0.66 A (0.9 V) and 0.81 A (1.2 V), i.e. 30–40 % of 2 A, as the datasheet designs for.
- **Input current at 4.6 V:** about **0.45 A typical, 0.7–0.8 A heavy** (~80–85 % efficiency). That's less than feeding the same board from the amp's 3.3V with linear core regulators (~0.8–1.0 A at 4.6 V), so it also relieves U3 and D5 (finding 5).
- **Sequencing:**
  1. The AP63203 (3.3 V) starts first (EN tied to VIN, 4 ms soft-start).
  2. EN of both AP63201 bucks comes from the 3.3 V rail through a divider (e.g. 10 k / 10 k → 1.65 V, so they enable once 3.3 V passes ~2.4 V). That gives **IOVDD before DVDD** (ADAU1466) and **VDD within 50 ms of VDDIO** (XU316).
  3. The 1.8 V LDO follows 3.3 V directly.
  4. **XU316 RST_N and ADAU1466 RESET** stay low until all rails are up: a small voltage supervisor on 0.9 V/1.2 V, ANDed with DSP_RESET from the ESP32 (U10 pin 3).
- **Input:** 47–100 µF bulk + ceramics at the connector. The bucks' 4 ms soft-start limits the inrush. Optional: a load switch at the input, enabled by an ESP32 GPIO, so the firmware can power-cycle the DSP board. Plug the DSP board in only with the amp board unpowered.
- **The amp board's 3.3V on U10/CN5** is then only a logic reference for the DSP board (I²C pull-ups R21/R22 are on the amp board), not its supply.

#### LC filter calculation (2026-11-25)

**Topology (from the KiCad netlist):** each of the four legs (A+, A−, B+, B−) is L → node, then C88–C103 from the node to **GND**. There is no differential cap between + and −. Per leg today: 1 nF C0G + 680 nF + 150 nF + 220 nF MKP, **all fitted = 1.051 µF**. The speaker sits across A+/A− (and B+/B−).

**Model:** for the differential (speaker) signal, a BTL filter with caps to GND is equivalent to one leg driving **R_load / 2**:

- f₀ = 1 / (2π √(L·C)) (per-leg L and C)
- Q = (R_load / 2) · √(C / L). Q ≈ 0.71 is Butterworth (maximally flat); a higher Q peaks, a lower Q droops.
- The values below are computed from the full transfer function, including the inductor's DCR. They don't include film-cap ESR, core loss, or the speaker's real (inductive, rising) impedance.

**Inductors:**

- XGL1712 and MA5172 give the same response within 0.4 kHz. Their only difference here is DCR: about 0.1 dB at 4 Ω, plus the open-load resonance peak (below).
- **No load (speaker unplugged):** the LC resonance is damped only by DCR and losses. In this model it reaches +56 dB (XGL1712) or +42 dB (MA517x) at f₀. In practice cap ESR and core loss lower it, but it's still large. Avoid driving ultrasonic content into an open output. If wanted, a Zobel network (series R + C across each output) bounds it.
- **Carrier attenuation:** ≈ 40·log(f₀ / 600 kHz). Each step up in C gains about 1 dB of 600 kHz suppression (EMI and residual carrier on the speaker cable).
- **Ripple current** in L at idle (rough, at 50 % duty): ΔI ≈ PVDD / (4·L·f_PWM) = 35 V / (4 · 10 µH · 600 kHz) ≈ 1.5 A pp for 10 µH and ≈ 2.1 A pp for 7 µH. Higher ripple means more idle loss and heat in the inductors and U12. 7 µH still meets TI's L ≥ 5 µH at I_OC (V1.3-4).
- **TI reference:** Figure 8-1 (BTL) uses 10 µH + 680 nF per leg, the first row.

**Reading the table:**
- **10 µH + 680 nF (TI's values):** the best compromise between 4 Ω and 8 Ω. −0.75 dB / +0.5 dB at 20 kHz.
- **10 µH + 1.05 µF (current BOM):** flatter at 4 Ω (−0.4 dB at 20 kHz) and 3 dB better carrier suppression, but 8 Ω speakers get +1 dB at 20 kHz and a +2.9 dB peak at 68 kHz.
- **7 µH (MA5173):** the flattest 4 Ω response (within ±0.2 dB to 20 kHz, −3 dB at 64 kHz). On the other hand: Q 1.25–1.55 at 8 Ω (+2.6 to +4.2 dB peak), ~40 % more ripple current, and less carrier attenuation.
- Rule of thumb: pick C for the **impedance of the speakers you'll actually use**. A real speaker's impedance rises at high frequencies (voice-coil inductance), so it behaves more like the 8 Ω rows than the 4 Ω rows above 10 kHz.

- If the MA5172 buzzes mechanically: fix it with a dab of glue or varnish rather than changing the part.
